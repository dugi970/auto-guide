Why Is My Steering Wheel Making a Whining Noise When Turning?

Published: Oct 2026 | Written by AutoGuide Editorial Team
Quick Answer:

A high-pitched whining or groaning sound when turning the wheel is most commonly caused by low power steering fluid or air bubbles trapped in the power steering hydraulic system. Other causes include a worn-out power steering pump, a slipping serpentine belt, or a damaged steering rack.

Top Causes of Whining or Groaning Steering Noises

Hydraulic power steering systems rely on fluid pressure generated by a pump driven by the engine belt. When fluid flow is disrupted, the pump suffers cavitation, producing a loud whining noise that pitch-shifts as you turn the steering wheel or step on the gas.

1. Low Power Steering Fluid

This is by far the most frequent issue. As power steering fluid leaks out through aging seals, hoses, or the rack, air gets sucked into the pump chamber. The pump struggles to pressurize air, creating a distinctive whining or buzzing sound when turning.

2. Failing Power Steering Pump

Internal vanes and bearings inside the power steering pump wear out over time. Even if fluid levels are correct, a failing pump cannot maintain smooth hydraulic pressure, generating a persistent whine that gets significantly louder at full lock (turning the wheel all the way left or right).

3. Slipping Serpentine Drive Belt

The power steering pump is driven by the engine's serpentine belt. If the belt is glazed, stretched, or the tensioner is weak, turning the wheel adds load, causing the belt to slip against the power steering pulley. This produces a squealing or whining noise.

4. Contaminated or Old Fluid

Power steering fluid breaks down due to high heat and collects metallic particles from normal wear. Old, oxidized fluid loses its viscosity and hydraulic effectiveness, causing pump friction and noisy operation.

How to Troubleshoot and Fix the Noise

  • Check the Reservoir Dipstick: Locate the power steering reservoir cap under the hood and check the dipstick level while the engine is cool. Top up with the recommended fluid type if low.
  • Look for Foaming Fluid: Remove the reservoir cap while the engine runs. If the fluid looks foamy or bubbly, air is being drawn into the system through a loose hose connection or shaft seal.
  • Inspect Under the Car: Check for reddish or clear hydraulic fluid puddles underneath the engine bay or near the front tires (steering rack boots).

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can electric power steering (EPS) whine when turning?

Vehicles with Electric Power Steering do not use hydraulic fluid. A whining noise in EPS systems usually indicates a failing electric motor or worn steering column bearings.

Q: Is it safe to drive with a whining power steering pump?

Short distances are manageable, but driving long with low fluid will permanently destroy the pump and risk sudden loss of power steering assistance while driving.
